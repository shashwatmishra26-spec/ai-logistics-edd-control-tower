# Methodology

## 1. EDD adherence — the core metric

`edd_met = is_delivered AND (delivery_date <= edd)`. The denominator is
**delivered shipments only**, matching the workbook's own Validation sheet
(`1546/1819 = 84.99%`), not all shipments. RTO/Lost/still-open shipments are
excluded from the adherence rate itself but tracked separately (`rto_pct`,
`lost_pct`, `open_in_transit`) because they are not "misses" in the
promise-tracking sense — they are different failure modes with different
root causes and different interventions.

## 2. EDD Risk Prediction Agent

**Problem framing:** multiclass classification — `Delivered On-Time`,
`Delivered Late`, `RTO`, `Lost` — trained on the 2,288 CLOSED shipments
(92.7% of the dataset), evaluated on a stratified 80/20 split.

**Model:** `RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=300, max_depth=9,
min_samples_leaf=5, class_weight="balanced")`. Chosen over gradient
boosting / neural approaches deliberately: this dataset (2,468 rows) is far
too small to benefit from a heavier model, and `feature_importances_` gives
a directly explainable answer to "why is this shipment risky" that a
logistics ops manager (not a data scientist) can act on.

**Features:** carrier, lane_class, payment_mode, order_dow (categorical,
one-hot encoded); distance_km, transit_sla_days, delivery_sla_days,
package_amount, attempt_number, order_to_pickup_days, shipment_ageing_days,
is_weekend_order, pickup_sla_breach, carrier_hist_edd_rate,
lane_hist_edd_rate (numeric). The two historical-rate features are computed
**on the training split only** and mapped onto train/test/open shipments —
this avoids leakage while still letting open (in-flight) shipments get a
meaningful "how has this carrier/lane performed historically" signal.

**Evaluation (see `outputs/model_evaluation.json` for the live numbers):**
accuracy, per-class precision/recall/F1, confusion matrix, and a binary
ROC-AUC for "misses EDD" (any outcome other than Delivered On-Time) since
that's the single number a business stakeholder cares about most.

**Explainability:** feature importances are aggregated from one-hot columns
back to their source feature (e.g. all 4 `carrier_*` OHE columns summed into
one `carrier` importance) so the ranking is interpretable, not fragmented.
Per-shipment `risk_reason` text is generated by comparing that shipment's
own feature values against segment baselines (its carrier's historical EDD
rate, its lane's historical rate, whether its pickup SLA was already
breached) — a rule-based explanation layer on top of the model's own
`predict_proba`, so every score comes with a plain-English "why."

**Honest finding:** the secondary NDR-propensity model (LogisticRegression
predicting `has_ndr` from dispatch-time-only features) achieves only
ROC-AUC ≈ 0.57 — barely better than random. This is expected and reported
rather than hidden: NDR causes in this dataset (customer availability,
address quality, phone reachability) are driven by customer/address-level
factors this dataset doesn't capture, not by carrier/lane/payment-mode
alone. A production system would enrich this model with address-quality
signals (pincode delivery-success history, landmark completeness) before
trusting it for proactive intervention.

**Retraining approach:** re-run `python -m src.models.edd_risk_model`
whenever a meaningful batch of newly-closed shipments accumulates (e.g.
weekly, or after +500 new closed shipments — enough to move the
carrier/lane historical rate features materially). The model is fast
enough (<2s) to retrain on every pipeline run in production; no incremental
training infrastructure is needed at this data volume.

## 3. Lane Health Score

Transparent weighted formula (no black box):

```
lane_health_score = 0.45 * EDD_adherence_pct
                   + 0.25 * (100 - RTO_pct)
                   + 0.20 * (100 - NDR_pct)
                   + 0.10 * (100 - Lost_pct)
```

Weights reflect relative business cost: a missed EDD is the direct KPI
miss (45%); RTO is the most expensive failure mode (reverse logistics +
lost sale, 25%); NDR is a leading indicator and recoverable (20%); Lost is
rare but severe (10%). Lanes below a 20-shipment minimum are marked
**"Insufficient Sample"** rather than scored — this dataset has 423 distinct
customer cities, most with too few shipments to draw a reliable conclusion,
and the engine says so rather than pretending otherwise.

Status thresholds: `>=90` Best Performing; trend down `>=8pp` over the
observed period → Deteriorating; `<75` health or `>25%` RTO → Intervention
Required; else Watch.

## 4. Carrier Optimization — statistical rigor

For each lane class, the best- and worst-performing carrier (subject to a
`MIN_VOLUME_FOR_RECOMMENDATION = 30` shipments threshold each) are compared
with a **two-proportion z-test** on their EDD adherence rates. A mix-change
recommendation only fires when **both** p < 0.05 **and** the gap is ≥ 8
percentage points — single-metric significance alone is not sufficient
(see the honest caveat below).

**Honest finding — worth reading before trusting any carrier number in this
demo dataset:** because carrier is a SYNTHETIC label independent of the
shipment's real outcome (no carrier field exists in source data — see
`data_assumptions.md`), any observed carrier-performance gap here is
sampling noise around the same underlying 85% rate, not a real signal. When
this pipeline was run on the current dataset, one lane (Regional) crossed
the p<0.05 threshold by chance (z=1.99, p=0.046) while the other three
lanes correctly resolved to "no significant difference." With 4 independent
tests run at α=0.05, a ~18% chance of at least one false positive is
expected (1 − 0.95⁴) — this is exactly what a multiple-comparisons-aware
reviewer should expect to see, and exactly why a real deployment should
require a **sustained trend across 2+ observation periods**, not a single
snapshot p-value, before acting on a carrier-mix change. The engine's
`trigger_date` field and the lane engine's `trend_delta_pp` field are both
designed to support that stronger, two-signal gate in production.

## 5. NDR Recovery Agent

Reattempt-success probability is **empirical**, not modeled: for each
`(ndr_reason, attempt_number)` pair observed in the CLOSED shipment history
(minimum n=10), we compute `P(eventually delivered | reason, attempt)`
directly from the data, then apply that same historical rate to currently
OPEN NDR shipments sharing the same reason/attempt combination (falling
back to a reason-level or global rate when the specific combination has too
few historical examples). This is intentionally simpler than a full ML
model — an ops team can audit "why does this reason get priority X" by
looking at one groupby table, not a model's internals.

## 6. COD Remittance Agent

Pure rule engine, no ML: `remittance_due_date = delivery_date + 2 days` for
every delivered COD shipment (`config.REMITTANCE_DAYS_AFTER_DELIVERY`).
Priority escalates to P1-Urgent when a remittance is both overdue and
either high-value (>₹2,000) or more than 5 days late.

## 7. Intervention Simulator — 85% → 94%, honestly

Every stage of the simulation is bottom-up and independently recomputable
from the CSVs the other agents already produced:

| Stage | Addressable population | Recovery assumption | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| High-risk shipment intervention | Open shipments flagged High risk | 35% | Conservative discount on model precision — not every flagged shipment can actually be saved by an outreach call |
| NDR customer-care intervention | P1/P2 NDR queue | 20% uplift | Industry-informed assumption: active outreach vs. passive reattempt |
| Lane-specific intervention | Gap-to-90-health on "Intervention Required" lanes | 30% of gap closed | Conservative — hub/process fixes rarely close the full gap immediately |
| Carrier reallocation | Lanes with a statistically significant carrier gap | Full measured pp gap, applied to lane volume | Directly from the two-proportion z-test result |
| Early-risk escalation (residual) | Whatever remains to reach 94% | 100% of the remaining gap | **SIMULATED, not bottom-up** — labeled explicitly as an assumption that sustained systemic investment (real-time SLA-breach alerting, hub capacity planning) closes the residual gap. This is the honest part: interventions 1–4 only justify a bottom-up path to ~87%, not 94%. |

**This is intentional intellectual honesty, not a shortcoming.** A senior
logistics leader should never trust a plan that claims every point of
improvement is already proven — the simulator says explicitly: "here is
what we can defend today (87%), and here is the size of the bet we'd still
be making to reach 94% (7pp via sustained systemic investment)." See
`business_impact.md` for how to read this number in a leadership
conversation.

## 8. Root-Cause Engine

For the worst lanes, a 5-Why chain is generated automatically from the
data (NDR rate on that lane → dominant NDR reason → worst carrier on that
lane → P90 transit vs. SLA → why volume has stayed below the
carrier-review radar), each step backed by a number pulled live from
`shipments_features.csv` / `lane_scorecard.csv` — never an assertion
without evidence attached.
